# 14. Notice immediate usefulness without inventing brain claims

## Aim

A routine is easier to evaluate when its immediate result is visible.

A modest satisfying consequence can be the action’s own benefit: a clear desk, a found key or a completed note. Keep rewards proportionate and avoid turning the plan into a costly incentive scheme. You do not need a story about brain chemicals to decide whether a practical consequence is helpful.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

Immediate satisfaction and longer-term value can diverge. An action may feel rewarding now but add little to the goal, or be useful while remaining effortful. Look for both rather than assuming enjoyment proves effectiveness. A simple visible consequence often gives enough feedback without adding purchases, competition or elaborate reward tracking.

## Fictional case

Mei puts keys in a bowl and notices that leaving home requires less searching.
