# 19. Decide whether to keep, change or retire a routine

## Aim

A routine is valuable because it serves a purpose, not because it has a long streak.

Review benefit, effort and fit together. A well-practised action can become unnecessary when the goal changes. Retiring it deliberately is different from judging yourself for inconsistency. Document the reason and identify whether any useful part belongs in a new routine.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

Maintenance decisions should account for duplication. Two systems that once served different purposes may now copy the same information, increasing effort and error. Identify what would actually be lost by removing one. A short trial of a simpler version can reveal whether the retired step had a benefit that was initially overlooked.

## Fictional case

Vic maintains a daily paper planner after switching to a shared schedule that already covers the same tasks.
