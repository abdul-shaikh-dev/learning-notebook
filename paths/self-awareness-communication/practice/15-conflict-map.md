# 15. Map positions, concerns and constraints

## Aim

A position is the proposed solution; the concern underneath explains why it matters.

Two people may propose different solutions while sharing part of the goal. List each account without declaring one complete story. Include non-negotiable constraints such as a deadline or available space. Understanding concerns can expand options; it does not guarantee agreement.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

A stated position may hide several concerns, so do not assume a single underlying need explains everything. Ask which parts are essential and which are flexible. A shared aim can help compare options but does not eliminate conflicting constraints. Preserve the disagreement when no feasible overlap exists instead of forcing a superficial agreement.

## Fictional case

One classmate wants an evening meeting; another insists on mornings because of caring responsibilities.

## Worked fictional response

Positions: evening versus morning. Concerns: finishing work first versus availability around care. Shared aim: enough time to prepare the presentation. An asynchronous outline plus a short overlap slot may address both more than arguing over a single preferred time.
