# 18. Choose a channel that fits the issue

## Aim

A channel affects how easily people can clarify meaning, prepare and keep a record.

Text can be useful for facts and confirmation; a scheduled conversation can help explore a complex disagreement. Preferences and accessibility matter, so ask what works. Do not assume an in-person discussion is always superior or that a short message has a hostile tone.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

Written messages preserve a record but remove immediate opportunities to clarify tone. Live conversations allow questions but may be inaccessible or difficult to schedule. A combined approach can work: exchange facts in writing, discuss the unresolved point in an agreed format, then record decisions. Ask about access needs instead of assuming one format suits everyone.

## Fictional case

A group chat disagreement grows as people answer different parts of a long thread.

## Your practice

Choose a next channel and write a neutral transition.

Observation / known facts: ________________________________________

Interpretation / uncertainty: _____________________________________

Proposed next action and timing: __________________________________

Constraint or exception: __________________________________________

Evidence to review afterwards: ____________________________________

## Worked fictional response

We seem to be discussing two questions at once: timing and workload. Could we use a shared outline and a ten-minute call, or written comments if that works better? Afterwards we can record the agreed tasks in one message.

## Self-check

- Channel choice considers access and clarification.
- The final decision will have an agreed record.
- Compare your answer with the example. Explain any different choice.
