# 10. Acknowledge experience while keeping your own view

## Aim

Acknowledging someone’s concern is different from accepting every claim or proposed solution.

Name what you have heard without pretending certainty about their feelings. You can understand why a delay matters and still disagree about who should do the task. This helps keep the practical disagreement visible instead of turning listening into a demand for agreement.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

Validation becomes misleading when it claims knowledge of an emotion the person has not named. Use tentative language and focus on the reported disruption. You can acknowledge the effect while declining an unworkable request. Keeping those functions separate helps avoid a promise made only to end discomfort in the conversation.

## Fictional case

Mo is upset that a shared room was unavailable and asks Pat to cancel another group’s booking.

## Your practice

Acknowledge the concern, then state what you can and cannot do.

## Worked fictional response

I hear that losing the room disrupted your plan. I cannot cancel their booking, but I can help check the spare room. Pat recognises the disruption and states what is actually possible.

## Self-check

- The disruption is acknowledged without guessing motives.
- The limit and available help are explicit.
- The worked response is used for comparison, with any justified alternative explained.
