# 13. Give feedback about a specific event

## Aim

Useful feedback describes an event, its effect and a possible next step.

Choose a relevant example and avoid global labels. Ask whether the timing is workable, especially when the feedback is not urgent. Describe your experience as your experience. The other person may have information that changes your interpretation or the proposed solution.

## Mechanism and practical trade-off

Feedback about an artefact is easier to revise when it identifies the exact location and effect. Avoid exaggerations such as everyone was confused when only two readers asked. A proposed change is a starting point for discussion: the author may know a constraint that suggests a different solution. Ask for that perspective before treating your fix as mandatory.

## Fictional case

A slide uses unexplained abbreviations, and two readers ask what the labels mean.

## Your practice

Write specific feedback that invites a response.

Observation / known facts: ________________________________________

Interpretation / uncertainty: _____________________________________

Proposed next action and timing: __________________________________

Constraint or exception: __________________________________________

Evidence to review afterwards: ____________________________________

## Worked fictional response

In the summary slide, ABC and QRT are not defined. I could not interpret the comparison until I asked. Could we add a short legend? Is there a reason the labels need to stay abbreviated? This points to a changeable artefact rather than judging the author.

## Self-check

- A specific artefact or event is identified.
- Impact, proposed change and response invitation are present.
- The worked response is used for comparison, with any justified alternative explained.
